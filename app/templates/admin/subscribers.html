Customers are worked out from your orders: anybody on this list whose address has paid for something is one, counted live so there is nothing to keep up to date. A refunded order stops counting. The star is for the ones this site never saw — a sale in person, an order by telephone, or somebody who signed up with one address and bought with another — and taking the star off somebody who HAS bought here leaves them a customer, because they are one. A newsletter can then be sent to everyone or to customers only.

To email this list, download it and use whatever you normally send email with. This site collects addresses and records consent; it deliberately isn't a mailing tool, and sending bulk email from your own mail account is how that account gets suspended.

If you are ever asked to account for this list

Nobody is on this list without having answered an email. The site records each step, and the spreadsheet above is the whole record, one row per person:

  • They asked. The date, the page they were on, the machine they asked from, and the exact wording they were shown — kept per person, not looked up later, because the sign-up block will be edited and the promise made to somebody last spring is the one that counts.
  • They were invited. The date the confirmation email was sent, stamped after the mail server accepted it.
  • They answered. The date they followed the link, and the machine they followed it from. Until that happens the address is never written to again — not a newsletter, not anything.
  • They left, if they did. The date. The row stays, which is what lets you show the mail stopped.

Every message sent after they confirm carries an unsubscribe link that needs no password, in the footer and in the message's own headers so their mail program can offer its own button. The first one goes out the moment they confirm.

Erasing somebody (the red ×) is a different act from unsubscribing them and removes the consent record along with the address — right for a person asking to be forgotten or an address that was never real, wrong as a way to stop the email, since it takes the proof with it.

This is the mechanism, not legal advice — what your particular business has to keep, and for how long, is worth a lawyer's ten minutes.
